# Question-type training

Organize practice around the kinds of questions that recur in the learner's supplied materials. Train the learner to recognize what is being asked, choose the method and do the work.

## Sequence

1. **Map recurring questions.** Group source questions by what they ask for. Record the source page or question, the method and the common confusions.
2. **Set priorities.** Order practice by how often a type recurs in the materials, confirmed exam scope and the learner's actual results.
3. **Learn the setup.** Connect what is asked with the relevant figures, rates, timing and method. Explanations stay optional; letters alone are accepted.
4. **Practice varied cases.** Use complete, realistic source problems. When a case is finished, vary figures, dates and conditions within the source's rules. Label adaptations and announce case changes.
5. **Mix types.** Once several types are known, present them together so the learner must choose the method instead of repeating one formula or answer pattern.
6. **Revisit later.** Check retention with fresh questions and no help.

## Rules

- One question at a time, with all givens, dates, conditions and original choices.
- Hide answers and deciding hints until commitment, unless teaching is requested.
- After a miss or "I don't know", give short, simple numerical feedback using the same case.
- Keep the case and its figures until finished. Prefer meaningful applications over repeated low-value checks.
- Train recognizing the requested amount as well as calculating it (for example, cash paid versus interest earned, or total cost versus expense for the period).

## Coverage

Frequency sets the order; results set the emphasis. Cover **100% of the planned scope**, including less frequent types. Coverage is separate from accuracy and memory.

How often a type appears in a worksheet is evidence about that worksheet, not about the real exam. Don't invent exam frequency rankings or percentages.
